The field of automatic text summarization has been dramatically revolutionized by Deep Learning and the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) like those based on the Transformer architecture (e.g., BERT and GPT). Traditionally, summarization was often extractive, meaning AI simply selected the most important sentences from the source text and strung them together. The recent breakthrough is the dominance of abstractive models, which no longer just copy phrases but instead generate entirely new, human-readable sentences that capture the core meaning. This requires a deeper, more contextual understanding of the source material, resulting in summaries that are more fluent, cohesive, and concise—almost as if a human wrote them. These new techniques allow for the efficient processing of massive amounts of data, from condensing lengthy legal documents and financial reports to summarizing multi-turn conversations in real-time customer service logs, fundamentally changing how individuals and businesses consume information.
